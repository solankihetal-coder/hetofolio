Project Overview

More than a storefront.

SavvyShopper is a herbal and Ayurvedic e-commerce project focused on making natural wellness products easier to discover and purchase.

The product range spans health and nutrition, skincare, personal care, home care, baby care, agriculture, veterinary products, and food.

My work explored two connected challenges: creating an approachable shopping experience and defining the systems needed to support a more capable e-commerce platform.

Project: SavvyShopper
Domain: E-commerce / Smart Shopping
My Role: UI/UX Design + Systems Analysis
Methodology: Waterfall SDLC

Beyond the interface

Alongside the UI, I explored product data, user requirements, database relationships, and the technical structure needed to support the platform.

The proposed expansion introduced ideas such as price tracking, personalized recommendations, and retailer integrations. These were explored as future system capabilities, not presented as completed features.

01

Background & Problem Definition

The challenge wasn't just selling products.

SavvyShopper's existing digital presence primarily introduced the brand and its products. The opportunity was to move toward a more structured shopping experience while addressing broader limitations in communication, reach, and performance measurement.

Where the existing approach fell short

01 — Unclear communication
Inconsistent messaging across channels made it harder to establish a cohesive brand experience.

02 — Limited reach
A narrow set of marketing channels restricted opportunities to connect with potential customers.

03 — Inefficient resource allocation
Without sufficient performance insights, allocating marketing budgets effectively was difficult.

04 — Limited analytics
The existing approach lacked robust tools for understanding user behavior and campaign performance.

05 — Outdated strategies
Traditional marketing practices created opportunities to rethink how the brand could engage digital shoppers.

The design opportunity

How might SavvyShopper create a clearer, more intuitive shopping experience while establishing the foundation for a scalable digital commerce platform?

02

The Design

A little nature. A lot of intention.

SavvyShopper began as an exploration of how thoughtful design could make herbal and Ayurvedic shopping feel simpler, more approachable, and trustworthy.

From brand identity to product discovery, I focused on creating a consistent experience that helps users find what they need without unnecessary friction.

Brand Identity

Wellness, without the visual noise.

A sage-green palette, nature-inspired visuals, and a hand-illustrated mascot establish a calm, approachable identity. These elements carry across the interface to create a consistent brand experience.

Homepage

A first impression that makes sense.

The homepage introduces the brand, communicates its value, and guides users toward product categories and featured items. The goal was to make exploration feel intuitive from the first scroll.

Category Page — Home Care

Less searching. More finding.

Product cards bring essential details together: name, price, description, and quick actions. Users can browse, compare, and move toward a purchase without unnecessary steps.

Login

Just the essentials.

A distraction-free login experience with minimal fields, a clear primary action, and a touch of brand personality.

Signup / Register

Getting started shouldn't feel like work.

A familiar form structure keeps registration straightforward and maintains consistency with the login experience.

What I'd Do Differently Now

Good design gets better when you question it.

SavvyShopper was my first complete UI/UX project, and revisiting it helped me recognize details I initially overlooked.

Content matters. Early product listings reused generic descriptions. I later replaced them with product-specific information to make browsing more useful and trustworthy.

Contrast matters too. Some category cards needed stronger text-to-background contrast. Today, I'd evaluate accessibility earlier in the design process rather than treating it as a final check.

The biggest lesson? A polished interface is only part of a good experience. The details need to work just as hard.

7.1 Development Methodology

Waterfall SDLC

The project followed a structured approach to documenting and developing the system.

01 — Requirement Analysis
Understanding business needs, reviewing existing processes, and defining system requirements.

02 — System Design
Planning workflows, database relationships, and system architecture.

03 — Development
Working with the selected development environment and database structure.

04 — Testing
Defining test cases, identifying issues, and checking system behavior.

05 — Maintenance
Planning for future fixes, updates, and improvements.

7.2 Feasibility Study

Could the proposed system work in practice?

The analysis considered four dimensions:

  • Technical: Availability of the required development tools and infrastructure.
  • Operational: Whether the system could be used without extensive technical training.
  • Economic: Potential implementation costs and available resources.
  • Behavioral: User adaptation and possible resistance to change.

The initial assessment indicated feasibility across these dimensions, subject to the assumptions documented in the project.

7.3 Fact-Finding Techniques

Understanding the system before defining the solution.

The analysis considered observation, interviews, record reviews, and document inspection to understand existing processes and information requirements.

These techniques informed the requirements documented for the proposed system.

System Design

The structure behind the shopping experience.

To understand how the platform could function beyond the interface, I documented its core data entities, relationships, and system events.

Explore the system design documentation

9.1 Entity Relationship Diagram

Connecting the pieces.

The proposed data model included five primary entities:

Customer — Account and contact information.
Product — Product details, pricing, stock, and imagery.
Order — Transaction and delivery information.
Order Item — Products and quantities within an order.
Membership Tier — Loyalty levels and applicable discounts.

Together, these entities establish the relationships needed for core shopping and order-management workflows.

Key Outcomes & Insights

What this project helped me understand.

01 — Product thinking goes beyond screens.

Designing the shopping interface was only one part of the challenge. Mapping user requirements and system relationships helped me understand how different product decisions connect.

02 — A good experience needs a solid structure.

Features such as product discovery, checkout, and membership benefits depend on how information is organized and managed behind the scenes.

03 — Small details influence trust.

Product descriptions, visual consistency, and readable text all contribute to how confidently someone can use an e-commerce experience.

04 — Future features need clear priorities.

The proposed smart-shopping capabilities opened up possibilities for personalization and price intelligence, but also reinforced the importance of defining what should be built first.

05 — Designing for growth starts early.

Thinking about modular features, data relationships, and integrations helped me approach the project as a connected product rather than a collection of individual pages.

11

Conclusion

More than a first project.

SavvyShopper started with a simple goal: design an approachable shopping experience for herbal and Ayurvedic products.

It became an opportunity to explore what happens beyond the interface — from product information and user flows to database relationships and system requirements.

Looking back, there are decisions I'd make differently. I'd prioritize accessibility earlier, validate assumptions more thoroughly, and keep the initial product scope focused before introducing advanced features.

But that's what makes this project meaningful to me.

It taught me to design beyond what people see and think about how the experience actually works.
